Following the Path of the Eagle

"Following the Path of the Eagle" is one of those books that challenges you to rethink how you see yourself. Bishop David Oyedepo uses the eagle as a powerful picture of what it means to rise above mediocrity and pursue a life of purpose and excellence.

The central message is simple but convicting: many of us settle for less than what we were created for simply because we've never been shown how to think differently.

What stood out most to me was how practical the book is. It's not just spiritual encouragement — it walks through real principles for breaking limiting mindsets, embracing vision, and pursuing growth with discipline.

As someone still early in my own journey, many of the chapters felt like they were speaking directly to where I am right now.

Atomic Habits

"Atomic Habits" breaks down behavior change into something genuinely actionable instead of vague motivation. James Clear's core idea — that tiny, 1% improvements compound over time — reframed how I think about discipline.

Instead of chasing big, dramatic goals, the book pushes you toward small, repeatable systems.

What I appreciated most was how practical it is. Every chapter ends with a clear takeaway you can apply immediately, not just theory.

This is one I'll be returning to often as I build better study and reading habits.

The 1% better every day idea genuinely changed how I plan my week.

"You do not rise to the level of your goals. You fall to the level of your systems."

The Alchemist

Paulo Coelho's "The Alchemist" follows a shepherd boy chasing a recurring dream across the desert. Beneath the simple plot sits a much bigger meditation on purpose, fear, and listening to your own life.

It reads quickly, but the ideas linger far longer than the story itself.

Some readers find the philosophy a little heavy-handed, and I understand that critique. But for me, reading it at this stage of life, as I figure out my own path, it landed exactly when I needed it to.

Crazy Love

Francis Chan's "Crazy Love" is a direct challenge to comfortable, lukewarm faith. It doesn't try to be gentle about it — the book confronts complacency head-on and asks hard questions about what a genuinely surrendered life actually looks like.

It's a short read, but a heavy one emotionally. I found myself pausing often just to sit with certain chapters before continuing.

Recommended if you're looking for something that will genuinely convict rather than simply comfort.

THE HONEST SHELF

We read it. You decide.

The Honest Shelf is a community reading journal built around one simple idea: your time is valuable, and every book deserves an honest opinion.

From Christian non-fiction and fiction to self-help and everything in between, we read widely and tell you what we really think.

No inflated ratings. No pretending every book is a masterpiece. No reviews written after reading the back cover.

Just real readers sharing what moved them, what challenged them, what didn't work — and whether a book is worth your time.
